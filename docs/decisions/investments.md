# Investments: decisions

Related: feature page [Investments](../features/investments.md); architecture [Investments](../architecture/investments.md).

## Current

Separate `InvestmentTransaction` ledger on ordinary accounts; FIFO cost basis; a corporate action that moves a holding is one entry naming both securities, its lots keeping their cost and acquisition dates, and a merger divides the cost by a typed or imported percentage, the rest realised against its cash, a spin-off moves a typed percentage of each parent lot's cost, 0% until set; a price per security and date with its source, written by hand, by the broker import, from a price file or, for held securities an administrator mapped, fetched daily from EODHD or Kraken once the administrator switches it on, a fetched price never replacing another source's; the portfolio's value over time computed from that history on request; Interactive Brokers Flex Query by upload and Flex Web Service; dividends and interest count as report and dashboard income, withholding tax and standalone fees as expense, never in budgets; realised gain is portfolio-only; options, futures and short positions are out of scope; a member may set target shares in one dimension, kept per member on the server, and the split of a new amount towards them is arithmetic computed in the browser, not advice

## Log

- **2026-10-02.** A spin-off is an entry on the parent naming the new security, whose cost share of every parent lot moves to a lot of the new security with the same acquisition date; the share is optional and counts as 0% until set, and the Interactive Brokers import, which carries no allocation, books spin-offs without one, finds the parent by the ISIN at the start of the row's description and asks for the share in the result (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: Requiring the allocation before an entry can be saved or imported; skipping imported spin-offs until one is typed; estimating the allocation from the two securities' prices on the first trading day; one lot of the new security dated on the spin-off instead of one per parent lot
  - Why: Issuers publish the allocation weeks after the event, often in a tax notice, and a daily sync cannot wait for a member, while the shares received are certain and belong in the holding at once. 0% understates nothing that is realised: the parent keeps its whole cost until the member sets the share, and the new shares show at no cost with "Cost share not set" beside them. A price estimate is what the issuer's figure replaces and the price history rarely has the first day. A lot per parent lot keeps the parent's purchase dates on the new shares, which the tax summary's holding periods depend on
- **2026-10-02.** A merger is one entry type for cash, shares and both: the shares given up leave first in, first out, a received security takes the cost share of their cost with their dates at the ratio received to given up, and the rest is a disposal against the cash. The cost share is a percentage on the entry, typed by hand, taken from the broker's value of the new shares on import, and otherwise left unset, which counts as 100%; an imported merger accepts a new cost share and nothing else (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: Separate cash-merger and stock-merger types; a sell plus a symbol change typed as two entries; dividing the cost by the market values the app finds in its own price history; a fixed rule such as gain recognised up to the cash; refusing a mixed merger without a value on import; keeping imported mergers read-only
  - Why: A merger paid in both is common and needs both halves on one date and from one lot set, so one type with optional cash and optional shares covers the three cases with one replay rule, and a two-entry booking could be half deleted. The price history rarely has the closing price of an acquirer's first day, and how the cost divides is set by each country's rules and the merger documents, so the app records the percentage it is given rather than computing one. 100% is the default because it never invents a loss and keeps every purchase date on the shares the member still holds; the import says which entries need a look. The broker cannot correct a figure it never sent, so the cost share is the one field of an imported entry a member may set
- **2026-10-02.** A symbol change is its own entry type that names the security the holding leaves and, in a new nullable `RelatedSecurityId`, the security it moves to; the replay moves the oldest lots, up to the shares moved, with their cost and acquisition dates. The holding checks replay the whole account instead of one security. An Interactive Brokers issue change (`IC`) becomes such an entry when its new row resolves to another security, and only updates the identifiers when it resolves to the same one (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: Editing the security's symbol and ISIN in place; two linked entries, a transfer out of one security and a transfer into the other; replaying only the securities a change touches; converting the cost when the two securities trade in different currencies
  - Why: An edit would rename every earlier trade and attach the old price history to the new symbol, and securities are installation-wide, so one member's rename would change everybody's history. Two entries could be deleted or restored one without the other and leave lots in neither security, while one entry moves them in one step and is undone in one step. Once lots cross securities, whether a sale is covered depends on entries of other securities, and the whole account is the smallest set that is always complete; it is one query of entries the import and the value chart already read. A cross-currency move would need an exchange rate the issuer never states, so it is refused with `holding.currencyDiffers` and such an import stays counted
- **2026-10-01.** A security's price is up to date when it reaches its source's target day: the last weekday before today for EODHD, yesterday for Kraken
  - Rejected: The last weekday before today for every source; a trading calendar per exchange
  - Why: Kraken has a close every calendar day, so the weekday target left Saturday's and Sunday's closes waiting until Tuesday's run. Exchange holidays already cost at most one call a day, so a calendar is not worth its upkeep
- **2026-10-01.** Target allocation is set in one dimension at a time, which the member chooses, and kept per member on the server in an `AllocationTargets` table that a `PUT` replaces whole; targets have no trash (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: Targets in every dimension at once; targets per browser in the preferences collection; a JSON column on the user row like the dashboard layout; one row per target with its own create, edit and delete and a trash entry; keeping the `GET` from API tokens like the dashboard layout
  - Why: Targets across dimensions conflict: 60% ETF and 50% USD can both be typed and both be impossible, so a chooser keeps the arithmetic answerable. Targets per browser would differ between the phone and the laptop of one person. The member import copies tables but not the user row, so a column there would be exported and never come back, while a small owned table goes through backups, the member export and the import with one line each, and its security keys are moved with the securities. Targets are edited together and must add up to 100, so replacing them whole is the natural write; deleting one would leave the rest not adding up, and removing them all is an empty list. The `GET` is readable with an API token, unlike the dashboard layout: targets are figures about money that a script comparing them with the portfolio needs, not a setting of the screen
- **2026-10-01.** Target shares add up to exactly 100 with at most two decimals, and a held bucket without a target counts as 0% rather than leaving an unassigned remainder (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: A total under 100 with the rest unassigned; spreading the remainder over the buckets without a target in proportion to their value
  - Why: The split needs a complete target to say where money goes. An unassigned remainder has no target to drift from and would make the typed shares mean something other than what was typed. A target of 0% is what "I want none of this" means, and it shows as drift above target
- **2026-10-01.** A new amount is split by filling the buckets furthest below their target, measured as value per point of target, up to one common level, in the browser, rounded down to cents with the cents left over going to the largest remainders (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: Splitting the amount by the target shares alone; giving the whole amount to the single most underweight bucket; minimising the squared distance to the targets; a server endpoint for the split
  - Why: Splitting by the targets alone ignores the drift and keeps an overweight bucket overweight, and one bucket at a time overshoots it with a large amount. Filling to a common level is the classic "contribute to the underweight buckets first": it never sells, never funds a bucket that would stay above its target, reaches every target exactly when the amount allows, and leaves each funded bucket at the same fraction of its target, which a member can check by hand. Squared distance gives a similar answer by a rule that is harder to explain. Every input is already on the page, so the answer follows each keystroke without a request, and rounding by largest remainders is how a household expense is already split in shares
- **2026-10-01.** The allocation section shows from the first valued holding rather than the second, and leaves closed positions out of the Security view (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: Keeping the section hidden below two holdings; keeping the zero rows of closed positions
  - Why: A member with one fund is the one who wants a target for a second, and the button to set it lives in the section. A closed position has no value to compare with a target, and `byType` and `byCurrency` already counted open holdings only
- **2026-10-01.** EODHD's quoted currency is stored on the security in a nullable `PriceQuoteCurrency` column, written by the first fetch and cleared with the mapping; Find's answers stay in memory until that first fetch stores one (decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed)
  - Rejected: A table of symbols and their currencies; Find writing the currency to the security; the save request carrying the currency the form chose; dropping Find's memory; keeping the currency in memory only, as decided on 2026-09-30
  - Why: Memory alone cost a second call per symbol after every restart, and on a 20-call day each restart could cost a tenth of the budget. One column on the row that already holds the mapping needs only one line of invalidation, in the mapper that clears the fetch result, which is smaller than the second copy the 2026-09-30 entry feared; a table would need its own invalidation and a cleanup of symbols nobody maps. Find answers for an ISIN before anyone picks a listing, so it has nothing to write to the security, and a currency sent by the browser would be trusted input for a check meant to catch a wrong listing. Dropping Find's memory would make every symbol chosen from Find cost two calls on its first fetch; keeping it costs nothing, and its loss on a restart costs at most one call per newly mapped security, once
- **2026-09-30.** Closing prices of held securities are fetched from EODHD with an administrator's API key and, for crypto priced in EUR, from Kraken's public OHLC endpoint, by the server, off until an administrator switches it on
  - Rejected: Yahoo Finance's unofficial chart API; Stooq; Alpha Vantage; Twelve Data, Finnhub, Tiingo, FMP, Marketstack and Massive; CoinGecko and Coinbase for crypto; OpenFIGI for the ISIN lookup
  - Why: Yahoo has had no official API since 2017, its terms forbid automated access and it broke again in September 2026. Stooq now needs a key obtained through a captcha and covers the EU patchily. Alpha Vantage allows 25 calls a day and 100 days of history, and the other free plans are US-only or tiny. EODHD covers EU exchanges, the US and funds by ISIN under a personal-use licence, and its free tier of 20 calls a day and a year of history fits a household portfolio. Kraken is documented, keyless, quotes in EUR and gives about two years of daily candles, where CoinGecko's free key needs attribution and a monthly quota. The lookup uses EODHD's own search, because that provider will be asked for the prices, and only when an administrator presses Find, because each lookup is one of the day's calls

- **2026-09-30.** A price carries its source, and a fetched price never replaces a typed, broker or file price of the same date; EODHD calls are counted per day on the settings row; a mapping is an administrator's choice, its key is kept like the SMTP password, and a quoted currency that differs from the security's is a failure
  - Rejected: Last writer wins; a cap per run; letting whoever creates a security map it; the key as a field of the general settings request; converting a price in another currency through exchange rates
  - Why: A broker mark and a typed price are the member's own statement of value, and a feed is a fallback. The job runs every six hours, so a cap per run would spend four times the free limit, while a counter survives restarts and also covers Fetch now and Find. A mapping decides what the server asks an outside service and spends the key, and securities are installation-wide, so only administrators change it, as with every other detail of a security since 2026-09-19. The general settings form sends the whole request and `GET /api/settings` is readable by every member, so the key has its own write-only route. A price in another currency means a wrong symbol, and converting it would hide that

- **2026-09-30.** EODHD's quoted currency comes from its search the first time a symbol is fetched and is remembered in memory, and a stale security is asked at most once a day
  - Rejected: Trusting the exchange suffix of the symbol; a column for the quoted currency; asking the search on every fetch
  - Why: The plan assumed the end-of-day answer names its currency, and it does not. The suffix does not decide it either: the London Stock Exchange lists the same fund in pence, pounds and dollars. A column would be a second copy of what the provider says and would need its own invalidation when the symbol changes, and asking every time would halve the free tier. Remembering it costs one extra call per symbol after a restart, which the budget counts. Asking a stale security again only on the next day keeps a holiday, when the provider has nothing new, from spending a call every six hours

- **2026-09-30.** Other brokers are read from one documented trade CSV, parsed into the Interactive Brokers statement model and booked by the same import
  - Rejected: A column mapping saved per broker like the bank CSV; a parser per broker's own export; a second import pipeline for CSV entries
  - Why: Broker exports differ in far more than column order (trades and cash in one file or two, signs, fees in their own rows), so a mapping would still need conversion in a spreadsheet, and a parser per broker is unbounded work without real samples. The statement model already carries everything the CSV can say, so one parser reuses security matching, the oversell check and deduplication unchanged

- **2026-09-30.** The portfolio's return is the money-weighted annualized rate over all cash flows of the chosen accounts plus today's value, computed per request by bisection, and allocation is answered per security type and currency by the server
  - Rejected: A time-weighted return; a return per holding; a simple total return without time; computing the slices in the browser
  - Why: A time-weighted return needs the portfolio's value on every flow date, which the price history only has where someone recorded a price, while the money-weighted rate needs only the flows the ledger already has and answers the household's question, what their money earned as it went in. One rate for the chosen accounts keeps the summary readable, a total without time misleads over years, and the security's type and currency are on the server already, so the browser does not repeat the grouping

Newest first. Each entry is a choice between real alternatives: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why.

- **2026-09-30.** The investments page renders the portfolio and the tax summary as two views, each with its own header, body and dialogs
  - Rejected: One page component that branches on the view for the title, the description, the header actions, the account filter, the error subject and the body; splitting out only the header actions
  - Why: Every part of the page differed between the two views, and the entry, import and securities dialogs open only from the portfolio. Two views hold one branch instead of six, and the dialog state lives where the buttons are
- **2026-09-21.** The yearly investment tax summary is printed from the page under a print stylesheet; there is no second PDF
  - Rejected: A MigraDoc PDF beside the transaction one; an HTML-to-PDF renderer in the backend
  - Why: MigraDoc lays the whole document out in memory, which is why the transaction PDF needs `App:PdfExportMaxRows`; a second MigraDoc document would mean laying the same tables out a second time, a second row cap and English-only text, because the PDF is written in the backend while this page is translated in the browser and formats money in the viewer's locale. Printing the page reuses the tables, the locale, the currency formatting and the frozen amounts already on screen, and is bounded by what one year of one caller's entries already put there. A second renderer would add a browser-sized dependency to the API image for one page
- **2026-09-21.** The tax summary CSV computes the year in memory and then writes its rows out, rather than streaming straight from a query
  - Rejected: Streaming rows from the database as the transaction CSV does; answering the JSON summary and building the CSV in the browser
  - Why: A first-in-first-out cost basis is not a property of the sell row: it needs every buy, sell and split of that holding from the beginning, so there is no query whose rows are the answer. The set is bounded by one year of one caller's entries, and the rows are still written to the response as they are produced, with no `Content-Length`, so the response shape stays the one the export decision of 2026-09-19 describes. Building the file in the browser would put money formatting and escaping in a second place and would make the export unavailable to anything that is not the page
- **2026-09-21.** The tax summary reports withholding tax and standalone fees as positive amounts paid, and applies no tax, rate or allowance of any kind
  - Rejected: Storing them with the ledger's own negative sign; computing a taxable base, an allowance or an amount owed for the Lithuanian declaration
  - Why: The sign matches what `PortfolioResponse.Years` already publishes, so a number on this page and the same number on the portfolio page cannot disagree, and "withholding tax: 4.44" reads the way a person filling a form reads it. Computing anything further would make the product state a tax position it cannot stand behind: rates, allowances and the treatment of a loss change by country and by year, and a wrong figure in this particular place is worse than no figure. The page and the documentation say plainly, in both languages, that it is a summary of recorded data and not tax advice
- **2026-09-21.** Account selection travels as one comma-separated `accountIds` query parameter, and an id the caller cannot see is dropped instead of refused
  - Rejected: A repeated `accountIds` key; answering 403 or 404 for an account the caller cannot see
  - Why: The comma-separated string is what `tagIds` already does and what the generated client puts on the wire anyway, and the CSV link is a plain browser navigation built by `buildExportUrl` rather than a client call. Refusing an unseen id would tell a stranger that an account with that id exists, which is exactly what the visibility filter is for; dropping it answers the caller's own accounts, which is what the default already does
- **2026-09-21.** `Position` now records the acquisition date of every lot and the lots each sale consumed; the tax summary reads them instead of a second replay of its own
  - Rejected: A separate first-in-first-out walk inside the tax summary service; storing disposals in a table when a sale is recorded
  - Why: Two replays of the same rule would eventually disagree, and the portfolio's realised gain and the tax summary's would be two numbers for one thing. A stored disposal would have to be rewritten whenever an earlier buy, sell or split is corrected or deleted, which the product allows, so the table would be a cache with no sound invalidation. The extra fields cost one `DateOnly` per lot and one list per sale in a replay that already runs per request
- **2026-09-20.** Reports and the dashboard read investment cash flows straight from the `InvestmentTransaction` ledger through `IInvestmentCashFlowService`: dividends and interest are income, withholding tax and standalone fees are expense, the sign of the entry is kept, and nothing is added while the `Investments` feature is off
  - Rejected: Mirroring every dividend into a `Transaction` row; classifying by the sign of the amount; counting realised gain as income
  - Why: A mirrored row would double the account balance or need a flag that every balance query must skip, and would have to follow edits, deletes, re-imports and revaluation. Classifying by type matches the portfolio totals, and net is the same. Realised gain depends on the cost-basis method and is not cash earned in the period, so it stays in the portfolio; trade commissions stay inside cost and proceeds as decided on 2026-09-19
- **2026-09-20.** Investment amounts appear in a category breakdown as an extra `CategoryBreakdownItem` with `categoryId` null and the new optional `syntheticGroup` (`investmentIncome`, `investmentTaxesAndFees`); the report gained `incomeByCategory` because the breakdown was expense-only and investment income had nowhere to go
  - Rejected: A separate `investmentIncome` and `investmentExpense` pair of numbers on the response; seeded system categories that the user could rename or delete; a localized name from the server
  - Why: Both changes are additions, so a client of the older shape still parses the response and treats the item like uncategorized spending with an English name. A pair of numbers would leave the breakdown not adding up to the total. The API does not know the viewer's language, so the client names the group from `syntheticGroup`
- **2026-09-20.** `SecurityPrice` keeps one price per security and date, and `Security.LastPrice` stays as the denormalized newest point that only moves when the written date is not older than the one already stored
  - Rejected: Replacing `LastPrice` with a lookup into the history; a price per user; recording every trade price as the price of its day
  - Why: Every holding value, account balance, dashboard total and net worth reads the last price, and a subquery per position on those paths costs more than one column that the single writer, `SecurityPriceBook`, keeps correct. A trade price is what one person paid in one moment, not the day's close, so recording it would move everybody's holdings to a stranger's fill
- **2026-09-20.** The portfolio's value over time is computed per request from the ledger and the price history, sampled daily up to 92 days, weekly up to 731 and monthly beyond
  - Rejected: Storing a daily portfolio value row; sampling daily over any range; reusing net worth snapshots
  - Why: A stored series would have to be rewritten whenever an entry, a price, a rate or the reporting currency changes, which is the same argument that keeps holdings uncached. Daily points over ten years is thousands of replays for a chart a few hundred pixels wide. Net worth snapshots start when the installation did, mix in accounts, assets and debts, and cannot be split back out
- **2026-09-20.** A point whose position has no price yet, no exchange rate, or an oversold replay is left out of that point's value and the point is marked `isPartial`
  - Rejected: Failing the whole request; carrying the last known value forward; showing the point as zero
  - Why: History that starts mid-way is the normal case for an imported account, so a hard failure would leave the chart unusable exactly where it is most wanted. A flag lets the client mark the incomplete part instead of drawing a line that silently understates or invents a value
- **2026-09-19.** Broker splits take their ratio from the `SPLIT n FOR m` text of the corporate action description; a split without that text is skipped and surfaces through the position check
  - Rejected: Deriving the ratio from the quantity change and the replayed holding; asking the user for the ratio during import
  - Why: The Flex row has no ratio field. The quantity change only gives the ratio when the application knows the whole holding, which is false for history that starts mid-way, and it would book a wrong ratio without any sign. The description states what the issuer announced. An import that stops to ask questions cannot run as the daily sync
- **2026-09-19.** Splits replay before the buys and sells of their own day
  - Rejected: Keeping creation order between buys and splits
  - Why: A broker import writes a split and the trades of its effective day with the same creation time, so the old order was undefined exactly where it mattered; trades on that day are in new shares
- **2026-09-19.** Shared securities: an administrator changes the details, and the last price is set through its own operation by an administrator or by a user who currently holds the security on a visible account; adding a security stays open to everyone
  - Rejected: Administrator-only for every write; a price override per user
  - Why: Any signed-in user could rename or reprice an instrument in everybody's portfolio. Administrator-only would break the "click a holding's price" flow for ordinary members, who are the ones who know the price. A per-user price needs a new table and a second valuation path through holdings, account totals and net worth for a value that is the same for every holder
- **2026-09-19.** The yearly and total Fees figure of the portfolio holds standalone fee entries only
  - Rejected: Adding the commission of every buy and sell to it as well
  - Why: A commission is already inside the cost basis or the proceeds, so it already lowers the realised gain; listing it again as a fee made the same money reduce the result twice
- **2026-09-19.** A collision on the unique security index during a broker import rolls the import back and runs it again, up to three times
  - Rejected: One installation-wide lock around security creation; catching the error only on the sync path
  - Why: The retry lives in `ImportAsync`, which upload and sync share, and also covers a security added by hand at the same moment. A global lock would serialize every import for a collision that almost never happens
- **2026-09-19.** No cache of holdings values across requests; one projected query per request and a request-scoped memo instead
  - Rejected: A memory cache keyed by account and invalidated on writes
  - Why: The value depends on entries, splits, security prices, broker imports, restore, the reporting currency and the newest exchange rate. Missing one invalidation would show a wrong balance, and money figures must not be stale
