# Households and sharing: decisions

Related: feature page [Households and sharing](../features/households-and-sharing.md); architecture [Sharing and households](../architecture/sharing.md).

## Current

### Sharing

Accounts, Categories and Tags, since 2026-09-30 Budgets, Goals, Recurring entries, Assets and Debts, and since 2026-10-02 [Transaction groups](transaction-groups.md), whose references must be shared with the same household and whose budgets count only the household's accounts; a shared asset or debt counts in full in every member's net worth, its valuations and payment links follow it, and a shared debt is paid only from accounts shared with its household; snapshots stay each member's whole net worth; record owner controls scope and deletion, and since 2026-10-04 only the owner is offered them, through `isMine` on every shareable response; account owner controls archiving; transfer changes require both accounts, and the new accounts as well when an edit moves it; a per-browser active household narrows the view to one household, defaults to everything, keeps personal records visible in every scope and never widens what a caller may see; it travels as the `X-Active-Household` header, or as `activeHousehold` on the three export links, is checked against the caller's memberships and is applied in `AppDbContext.ShareableFilter`; a transfer between accounts of two households is visible under either and changed only under Everything; reminders of a shared recurring entry still go to its owner

### Member removal

Other owners' account histories disappear even if the removed member entered transactions; the removed member's own shared accounts and categories become personal again; personally owned records remain accessible

### Settle-up

Splits and payments are household rows (`IHouseholdScoped`) with their own query filter; only a positive expense on an account the caller owns is split, whole and once, equally, by whole weights or by exact amounts; a split keeps its snapshot when the transaction changes until "Update split", and stops counting when the transaction is deleted; splits and payments go to the trash and the household log, and deleting a payment leaves its transfer; a split copies the transaction's date, description and amount and stores each member's amount once, by largest remainder; balances are derived on read per household and per currency and never converted; a payment writes a transfer only through `ITransferService` between accounts the recorder can see; the month-end close is unchanged, net worth counts the open balances only for a member who turns on Open balances, and since 2026-10-02 the per-browser My share choice, in Settings › Personal › Appearance since 2026-10-03, sends `share=mine`, so reports, personal budgets and the dashboard count a split at the member's own share; the payer changes a split, either party a payment; removing a member is never refused over an open balance

### People outside the household

Since 2026-10-01 a member keeps people without a login as personal `Contact` rows, splits an expense they paid with them (`ContactSplit` with one `ContactSplitShare` per person, the member's part on the split) and records payments either way (`ContactPayment`); balances are derived on read per person and currency with the settle-up allocation and balance code; payments write nothing to any account; a transaction is split once, with a household or with people; the balances stay out of reports and enter net worth only for a member who turns on Open balances, and My share counts a split with people at the member's own part; nothing of it is shareable or audited; `Households` gates it and it lives on the Households page and the ledger row menu Since 2026-10-03 the routes are behind their own `People` switch, which counts as on only while `Households` is on.

## Log

Newest first. Each entry is a choice between real alternatives: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why.

Older entries are in the git history of this file (`git log -p -- docs/decisions/households-and-sharing.md`).

- **2026-10-04.** As the owner decided, the screens offer the owner-only actions of a shared record — delete, archive, ungroup and the Visibility field — to its owner alone. Every shareable response, and the ledger's group summary, carries `isMine`, computed on the server for the caller; a member keeps editing the other fields, and the server's 403 `access.forbidden` stays as the safety net
  - Rejected: Showing the actions and explaining the refusal in the delete dialog, as before; a read-only Visibility field for members; `ownerId` on every response, compared with the signed-in user in the browser
  - Why: A member only learned of the refusal after asking for it. The "Shared · household" tag already tells them the record is shared and with whom, so a disabled field would repeat it. A boolean answers the one question the screen asks without shipping user ids it has no other use for and without every list needing the current user; accounts keep `ownerId` because settle-up picks another member's accounts by it
- **2026-10-03.** Money with people has its own `People` switch, read as `Households && People`, and the People section stays on the Households page
  - Rejected: Keeping it under `Households` as decided on 2026-10-01; an independent switch with a page of its own; checking two features on every gated route
  - Why: The owner wanted it switchable without turning households off. The section has no other home, and the product keeps features on the page where the user already is, so the dependency lives in `FeatureFlags.IsEnabled` and every gate, job and trash kind keeps reading one feature

- **2026-10-03.** As the owner asked, the My share choice moved from a toggle in the header of the dashboard, budgets and reports pages to a "Split expenses" section in Settings › Personal › Appearance, still per browser
  - Rejected: Keeping the header toggle, restyled; always counting at the member's share with no choice; removing the feature
  - Why: The owner did not like the button on three page headers. The choice is set once and rarely changed, like hidden amounts and rows per page, which already live in Appearance
- **2026-10-02.** As the owner asked, superseding the 2026-09-29 and 2026-10-01 answers that open balances stay out of net worth: a member may count their open settle-up balances, with households and with people, in their net worth. It is an opt-in per member, `AspNetUsers.CountOpenBalancesInNetWorth`, off by default and switched by "Open balances" on the net worth page through `PUT /api/networth/open-balances`. Positive balances count as a receivable inside assets and negative ones as a payable inside debts, per currency at today's rate, and snapshots take them from the day the choice is made. The choices inside it were decided while the owner was away; review them
  - Rejected: Counting them for everyone; a per-browser preference; netting all balances into one figure; separate snapshot columns for the receivable and the payable; rewriting earlier snapshots
  - Why: An opt-in keeps every existing figure unchanged unless a member asks, which the 2026-09-29 reasoning still argues for by default: a claim on a person is not an asset everyone wants to value. The hourly snapshot job runs without a browser, so the choice must live with the member, and the user row already holds the member's own settings. A balance with Ona and one with Jonas are a claim and a debt with different people, so they are kept apart, as an asset and a debt are. Folding them into assets and debts keeps the snapshot's parts adding up to its total without a migration of the snapshot table or the composition chart, and the response names the two amounts so the page can show them as their own lines. A snapshot records what the total was on its day
- **2026-10-02.** As the owner asked, superseding the 2026-09-29 answer that there is no "my share" view: reports, budgets and the dashboard can count a split expense at the member's own share. A per-browser toggle, `myShare` in `jx-preferences`, sends `share=mine` with the report, budgets and dashboard figure reads, and the server counts a split the member paid at their part (`SharedExpenseShare` or `OwnAmount`) and a household split another member paid at the member's share. `ShareSlices.OfAsync` turns each split into correction slices that the spending read paths append the way they append spread slices: the share minus the reporting amount where the member sees the transaction, spread like it, and the share alone on the split's date, in the transaction's category only when the member can see that category, where they do not. Household budgets, budget alerts, limits from history, the month-end close and the digest count in full. The choices inside it were decided while the owner was away; review them
  - Rejected: A stored per-member setting; computing the share in the browser; counting a non-payer's share only when they can see the transaction; dating that share on the transaction and reading its spread, tags, payee and place; applying the share to household budgets; subtracting settlement payments from spending
  - Why: Hidden amounts set the pattern for a view preference of one browser, and a query parameter puts the choice into the query key, so both views cache side by side and the existing invalidation rules reach them. The browser has only totals and cannot move a category or a trend point without the splits. The 2026-09-29 worry was counting twice in a household-scoped report; replacing each visible split row by the viewer's share, rather than subtracting the other members' shares, counts every row once whatever the scope. A share of a split paid from someone else's personal account is still the member's spending, which is the point of the view; its date, amount and description are what the household card already shows them, and the category is used only when it is shared with them, so nothing more of a personal transaction reaches them. A household budget is one figure for every member by the 2026-09-30 decision. A settlement moves money between members and is not spending, and the share already is what the member spent
- **2026-10-01.** The rule that a transaction is split once, with a household or with people, is held by the transaction-scoped advisory lock keyed on the transaction id: both create paths and the trash restore of either split take it inside a database transaction before `SplitRules.IsSplitAsync` looks, and the unique index of each table still answers a race within one kind.
  - Rejected: A table of split transactions with one unique `TransactionId` that both kinds write to; a database trigger that checks the other table; running the two creates at serializable isolation
  - Why: The two kinds live in separate tables, so no single unique index covers both, and a third table or a trigger would add a migration and a second place that knows about splits. Serializable isolation would need a retry on every serialization failure. The advisory lock is the helper goal progress and attachment uploads already use, costs one statement and serializes only requests for the same transaction
- **2026-10-01.** People outside the household are personal `Contact` rows with a name; an expense is split with them through `ContactSplit` and `ContactSplitShare`, the household split's shape, with the member's own part stored on the split (`OwnAmount`, null when they take no part, and `OwnWeight`) and the allocation done by `ShareAllocator` with the member listed first; payments are `ContactPayment` rows with a direction. The balance sum moved from `SettleUpService` into `SettleUpBalances`, which both features call; `SettleUpPlanner` is not used, because with two parties per person the fewest payments are the balances themselves. Decided while the owner was away; review it
  - Rejected: One `ContactShare` row per person and transaction carrying the method and the copy, with no parent; a split with exactly one person; letting contacts be members of a household without a login; shareable contacts
  - Why: A shared dinner has several friends on one bill, and equal or weighted parts are computed across everyone at once, so the parts need one parent that holds the method, the copy and the member's part, which is what the household split already has. A member without a login would reach the membership checks, the activity log and the shared filters, which all assume a user. A friend is the member's own relationship, and the brief was personal only
- **2026-10-01.** A payment with a person stores only the payment: it never writes a transaction or a transfer and is not linked to a ledger row. A loan that already sits in the ledger as an expense, such as a bank payment to the friend, is recorded by splitting that expense with the person and leaving oneself out; a loan with no ledger row, or a repayment, is a payment recorded from the People section. Decided while the owner was away; review it
  - Rejected: Writing an expense or income on one of the member's accounts with the payment; linking a payment to an existing ledger row with a unique index and a ledger mark; a dedicated "Loans" category the app creates
  - Why: The money the bank moved is already in the ledger from the import, so writing a row would count it twice, and an app-made expense or income would decide for the member how a loan counts in reports. A link needs a unique index, rules about the row's type and currency and a mark in the ledger for a convenience the split with oneself left out already gives an expense row. How the member categorizes the bank row stays theirs
- **2026-10-01.** Balances with people stay out of net worth, reports, budgets, the dashboard and the month-end close, as household open balances do. Decided while the owner was away; review it
  - Rejected: Counting what people owe as an asset and what the member owes as a liability in net worth
  - Why: The same reasoning as the 2026-09-29 settle-up answer: net worth moves when money changes hands, and an open balance is a claim on a person that the installation cannot value. A member who wants a large loan in net worth can still record it as an asset by hand
- **2026-10-01.** A transaction is split once, with a household or with people, never both; deleting a person is a soft delete that hides their balances and history and is restorable, and the retention purge takes their payments and shares with them and a split left with nobody; saving a split again drops a deleted person's share; the routes are under the `Households` switch, the People section sits on the Households page and the action in the ledger row menu, the two `GET` routes are token-readable and nothing is audited. Decided while the owner was away; review it
  - Rejected: Both splits on one transaction; refusing to delete a person with history or an open balance; a feature switch or a page of its own; logging the changes in the household activity log
  - Why: Two splits would let the household shares and the people's shares each add up to the whole amount, counting the expense twice. Undo is the rule for every record, and a person one no longer sees should be removable without settling first. The product direction keeps features on the page where the user already is, and the Households page is where shared money lives. The activity log records what is shared into a household, and people are personal
