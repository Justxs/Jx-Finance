# Dashboard: decisions

Related: feature page [Dashboard](../features/dashboard.md).

## Current

Per user, on the user row as a nullable `jsonb` column of card id strings; the published `DashboardCard` enum is the id set; unknown ids are dropped on read and cards a layout does not mention are appended in default order; feature switches are applied by the client and never rewrite the stored layout; hidden cards neither render nor load; reordering is by move up and move down buttons, no drag and drop. The page shows one month, the `month` search parameter, titled by its name with fixed previous, next and "This month" controls; the summary figures, trend, spending breakdown and pace follow it, the total balance is the balance at the month's end (today's on the current month), and on an earlier month every other card shows that month or its last day: budgets and account balances as of that day, net worth history up to it, the month's latest transactions; upcoming bills, the cash-flow card, added 2026-09-29, and the goals card, added 2026-10-01 as the last card id, show only on the current month. The budgets card lists every budget in its window, most used first. An earlier month shows its month-end close panel above the cards; the current month shows the close prompt for the latest ended month

## Log

Newest first. Each entry is a choice between real alternatives: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why.

- **2026-10-01.** A `goals` card lists every goal on the current month, appended as the last card id behind the `Goals` switch; the budgets card drops its five-row cut-off and lists every budget in its window
  - Rejected: Showing today's goal progress on an earlier month; working out a funded goal's progress at the month's end; a dashboard-only goals endpoint; capping goals at five like the budgets were; keeping the budget cap with a "more" row
  - Why: A manual goal stores only its current amount, so an earlier month could show only today's figure under a past month's title, which the 2026-09-28 entry already rejected; a past-month value for funded goals alone would make two kinds of row mean different things. The goals page's `GET /api/goals` already answers everything the card shows, so the card shares its query. A household has few goals and budgets, and the owner wanted to see all of them on the dashboard; the card grows instead of hiding rows

- **2026-09-28.** Spending pace projects the current month to its end: today's total, plus upcoming expense bills on their due days, plus the average's remaining spending less those bills, spread along the average's curve
  - Rejected: A straight line at the month's daily rate so far; the average's remaining spending alone; adding upcoming bills on top of the full average; a new forecast endpoint
  - Why: The month-to-date rate is skewed by rent and salary-day spending at the start of the month. The average alone ignores bills the user has told the app about, and adding them to the full average counts them twice, because the average already holds the same bills from earlier months. Subtracting them keeps the total honest while placing them on the right day. Everything needed is already in the browser, so a server forecast would duplicate it

- **2026-09-28.** Spending by category always compares with the same period of the previous month (the same days on the current month); spending pace compares with the average of the three previous months; net worth shows the shown month's change; the ring says "kept so far" on the current month; Spending by category goes wide when Spending pace is not shown
  - Rejected: A `compare` switch on `category-breakdown`; comparing the current month with the whole previous month; a new dashboard endpoint for the pace baseline; a single previous month for the pace; hiding the ring until the month ends; limiting the net worth chart to twelve months; dense grid packing for the missing pace card
  - Why: The dashboard is the endpoint's only caller and always wants the comparison, so a switch would be an unused branch. A partial month set against a full one always looks cheap, and the report's `ComparisonWindow` already cuts the same period. The report's daily buckets stop at 62 days, so three months need three requests, which the existing report endpoint and cache already serve; one month was too sensitive to which day rent fell on. The ring is still right on the current month, only incomplete, so a caption says so. The month's change ties the net worth card to the shown month without hiding the longer history. Dense packing would reorder the cards visually against the reading order, which was rejected before; widening one card fixes the default layout without it
- **2026-09-28.** The current month's bars in Income vs. expenses are faded, balance by account keeps the largest balances by size with an "other accounts" row, and budget meters on the current month carry a tick at the share of the window that has passed
  - Rejected: Leaving the current month's bars like the others; hatching them; Recharts `Cell` for the faded bars; sorting accounts by signed balance; dropping accounts past six without a note; colouring a budget ahead of the calendar instead of marking where the calendar is
  - Why: A month that has not ended looked like a fall in income and spending. Hatching needs an SVG pattern per theme for the same message opacity gives, and `Cell` is deprecated in Recharts 3 in favour of `shape`. Sorting by signed balance cut the most negative accounts first, which are the ones a person most needs to see, and the rows did not add up to the total. A budget colour for "ahead of pace" would add a third state to a meter that already has two, while a tick lets the reader compare bar and calendar themselves

- **2026-09-28.** The summary's total balance is the balance at the end of the shown month: rows dated on or before its last day, valued at that day's exchange rates and security prices; the current month, which has not ended, keeps today's balance, the same figure as the accounts page. This replaces the earlier choice of today's balance on every month
  - Rejected: Keeping today's balance labelled "Total balance today"; reading it from net worth snapshots; a separate as-of-date balance calculation for the dashboard; treating an account as opened on its `CreatedAt`
  - Why: Next to a past month's income and expenses a present-day balance reads as that month's. The reason for keeping it, that balances are not stored per date, did not hold: an account balance is already summed from its rows on every request, `AccountMovements` already takes an end date for the statement import's closing balance, exchange rates are kept per day and every security price is kept in `SecurityPrices`, so passing one date through the existing `AccountService` and `HoldingsValuation` path is small and cannot drift from the accounts page. Net worth snapshots start on the day the snapshot job first ran for a user, since no historical points are invented, and they include assets and debts. Accounts have no opening date and the starting balance is the balance before the first recorded row, so it counts in every month; `CreatedAt` is when the account was typed in, often after back-dated history, and would drop real balances. A holding with no price on or before the month's end is left out and the summary says it is incomplete, the same contract as today's total
- **2026-09-28.** The dashboard shows one month at a time, chosen with previous and next buttons and kept in the `month` search parameter; the month is the page's `h1`, and the navigation group sits at the end of the title row with "This month" invisible but keeping its space on the current month, and steps are debounced by 300 ms before the URL changes
  - Rejected: Keeping "Dashboard" as the title with the month as a description; placing the arrows right after the month name; removing "This month" from the markup on the current month; showing it disabled there; navigating on every press; reusing the month-close strip of twelve months; keeping the month in component state
  - Why: The month is what the page is about, so it is the heading. Arrows placed after the name moved as the name changed length ("May" against "February"), and a button that appeared and disappeared shifted them again; a fixed group at the end of the row stays put, and a disabled "This month" read as a control that should work. Navigating on every press fetched every month the user passed through; the debounced draft moves the heading at once and loads only where they stop. The twelve-month strip is built for the close workflow's statuses and is too heavy above the cards. A search parameter makes a month linkable and lets the route loader warm its queries
- **2026-09-28.** Every card follows the shown month: on an earlier month, budgets and balance by account ask for that month's last day through a new optional `asOf` on `GET /api/budgets` and `GET /api/accounts`, net worth history stops at that day, recent transactions are the latest of that month, and upcoming bills say they are shown on the current month. This replaces keeping those cards on today, chosen earlier the same day
  - Rejected: Keeping the present-tense cards on today while looking back; hiding them on an earlier month; new dashboard-only endpoints for past budgets and balances; a server-side cut-off for the net worth history; projecting past bill occurrences from each entry's cadence
  - Why: A page titled with a past month that shows today's budgets and balances next to that month's income reads as one month and is not. The budget calculator and the account balance already take a date (the month review and the month-end total use them), so an optional `asOf` on the existing lists reuses them without a second calculation, and leaving it out keeps every other page's query and cache as they were. The net worth history is one small query for all dates, so filtering it in the browser shares it with the net worth page. Recurring entries keep only their next due date, so the bills of a past month could only be guessed; the card keeps its place and says so rather than disappearing and moving the grid

- **2026-09-21.** The dashboard layout is a nullable `jsonb` column `DashboardLayout` on `AspNetUsers`, holding the order and the hidden set as card id strings
  - Rejected: A `DashboardLayouts` table keyed by user; two `text[]` columns; storing it per browser in a TanStack DB collection like saved filters
  - Why: The only existing per-user preference, `BillReminderEmails`, is a column on the user row, and a layout has exactly one owner and no identity of its own, so a table would add a key, a foreign key and a join for one value. One `jsonb` value is written and read whole, as it is used, and a second list later (a width per card, say) is a field rather than a migration. Per-browser storage was what the backlog asked to avoid: the layout is meant to follow the person to a phone. Strings rather than the enum's numbers keep a stored layout readable when a card is added or removed
- **2026-09-21.** Unknown card ids in a stored layout are dropped on read, and a known card the layout does not mention is appended after the saved ones in default order; a request with an unknown or repeated id is refused with `dashboard.cardUnknown` or `dashboard.cardDuplicate`
  - Rejected: Failing the read of a layout with an unknown id; inserting a new card at its default position between the saved ones; accepting and dropping unknown ids on save
  - Why: A layout written by another version must never break the dashboard, and the read cannot ask the user anything. Inserting a new card between cards the user arranged would quietly change an order they chose, while the end of the list is predictable and makes the new card noticeable. On save the client is the one that picked the ids, so an unknown one is a bug worth a field-level answer rather than silence; the request takes strings instead of the enum for exactly that reason, since an unknown enum value fails deserialisation with a whole-body `request.malformed`
- **2026-09-21.** Feature switches are applied by the client, and a card whose feature is off keeps its stored place and hidden flag; moving a card steps over such cards without moving them
  - Rejected: Filtering switched-off cards out on the server; dropping them from the saved layout when the user saves
  - Why: Switching a feature off deletes nothing anywhere else in the product, and the layout should behave the same: when the feature comes back, so does the card, where the user left it. The client already reads the switches for the navigation and needs the card list to render, so a second copy of the mapping on the server would only be another place to keep in step
- **2026-09-21.** The route loader reads the layout and the settings in parallel and warms only the queries of the cards that will be shown; the customise mode replaces the grid instead of overlaying it
  - Rejected: Warming every card's queries as before and only hiding the markup; editing the layout in place on the live grid
  - Why: Hiding the markup of a card whose data is still fetched saves nothing, and the point of hiding a card on a slow phone is not to pay for it. Editing on the live grid would mount every card, hidden ones included, just to offer them, and would move charts under the pointer while the user is still choosing
- **2026-09-21.** Cards are reordered with Move up and Move down buttons on each row; there is no drag and drop
  - Rejected: Adding `@dnd-kit` with its keyboard sensor; drag and drop only
  - Why: Nine rows are few enough that two buttons per row are quick, and buttons work the same for a pointer, a keyboard, a touch screen and a screen reader without a second interaction model or a new dependency. Focus stays on the pressed button, or moves to the opposite one when the card reaches an end, and a polite status says the new position, which is the part a drag-and-drop library would have had to be configured to do anyway
- **2026-09-21.** Saving or resetting the layout writes the answer into the query cache and invalidates nothing; both mutations are listed in `mutationsWithoutInvalidation`
  - Rejected: Invalidating the layout query after either mutation
  - Why: The response is the complete new layout and no other query depends on it, so a refetch would only repeat what was just answered and, in the meantime, show the old order for a moment after the customiser closes
- **2026-09-20.** The dashboard month figures, monthly trend and spending breakdown include the same investment flows as the report
  - Rejected: Leaving the dashboard on transactions only
  - Why: The dashboard month and the report for that month are the same question and showed the same number before; two answers would read as a bug. Budgets stay on categorized transactions because a budget belongs to a category, and the CSV and PDF exports stay transaction exports
