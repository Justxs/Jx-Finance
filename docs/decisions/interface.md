# Interface and command palette: decisions

Related: feature page [Interface](../features/interface.md); architecture [Visual system and motion](../architecture/visual-system.md), [Accessibility and keyboard shortcuts](../architecture/accessibility.md).

## Current

### Design

Neutral surfaces, compact controls, readable typography, semantic colors, mobile navigation; no external fonts. Two-way choices are segmented controls and category pickers are searchable comboboxes. Active ledger filters show as removable chips above the rows. Statement import opens from the ledger header and account rows as well as Settings. Amounts can be hidden per browser: the formatter hooks replace the digits with `•••••` and keep the sign and the currency, while inputs and raw text keep their values. Buttons with a text label are outlined unless they are the primary or a filled destructive action, and ghost buttons are icon-only. A row with three or more actions has one menu button; form actions in a dialog stick to the bottom of its body; column filters hold a draft applied with Apply; the pager states the row range and total and adds a page number field from five pages on; a ledger row's category changes in its cell; the accounts table carries each account's share and a Total row instead of a separate panel. Every signed-in route has a pending skeleton that mirrors its page, and stale content dims after 150 ms with `aria-busy`. Shared surfaces are React components and `cva` variants built from Tailwind utilities, with no `@layer components`. Per-browser preferences are one `jx-preferences` row in a TanStack DB local-storage collection

### Navigation

At most eight sidebar entries: Dashboard, Transactions and Accounts on their own, the hubs Categories (Categories, Tags, Rules), Plan (Budgets, Goals, Recurring entries), Wealth (Net worth, Investments) and Reports (Overview, Month close), and Settings at the bottom. A hub's pages keep their own routes and shortcuts and appear as tabs under the hub's title. Settings is one page for everyone over `/profile`, `/households`, `/users` and `/settings`, with a section nav grouped into Personal, Shared and Installation (administrators). The sidebar foot keeps only the notification bell, the collapse control is a handle on the sidebar's right border, and language, theme, keyboard shortcuts and sign-out are in an account menu on the user tile

### Command palette

One dialog on `Mod+K` over every page, the only shortcut with a modifier and the only one that fires while a field has focus: the pages and sections from a table beside the entries, the accounts, categories and tags from the lists the client already holds, and the actions that exist today, each row gated by the caller's role and the installation's feature switches. Matching is case- and accent-insensitive and fuzzy, ordered by how close the match is and then by what was used recently; the last eight choices live in `jx-preferences`. It loads its four lists when it first opens, never on page load, and adds no endpoint. Typing an amount and a payee offers quick add, parsed in the browser, which opens the ordinary add dialog filled in with an expense for today on the account this browser last used, and the category from a rule, the import's recall or a learned guess, in that order. Any text also offers a last row that opens the ledger searched for it

## Log

Newest first. Each entry is a choice between real alternatives: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why.

Older entries are in the git history of this file (`git log -p -- docs/decisions/interface.md`).

- **2026-10-04.** The signed-out landing and features pages take four exceptions to the design system: a full-width navy band, serif display headlines above the lead-figure scale, lifted showcase cards with a shadow, and one orchestrated load animation. The app itself keeps the flat, paper-first rules.
  - Rejected: Staying inside the system (paper ground, navy only on controls, the headline capped at the lead figure, one flat sample panel); a separate marketing look with its own colours and fonts
  - Why: The owner found the in-system page too weak to persuade a stranger. The exceptions reuse the brand's own navy and paper pairing (the app icon), its serif and its real components, so the pages still read as Jx Finance while giving the first screen a peak; they are scoped to the two pages by `LandingShell`, `on-hero` and `ShowcaseCard`.
- **2026-10-04.** A signed-out visit to `/` shows a landing page, served by its own public route `/welcome` and reached through a route mask, so the address stays `/`; a signed-out visit to any other private page still goes to `/login`.
  - Rejected: Rendering the dashboard or the landing page from the `/` route by session state; redirecting guests to a visible `/welcome`; keeping `/` a redirect to `/login` and linking the landing page from the sign-in card
  - Why: The root layout and the splash that covers crossing the sign-in boundary decide the side of a page by its path, and `/` on both sides would break that check on sign-out. A visible `/welcome` puts a second address on the front door, and a link from the sign-in card hides the page from the strangers it is for. The mask keeps every path on one side of sign-in and the front door at `/`.
- **2026-10-03.** The command palette's search box and list are Base UI's `Autocomplete`, inline and always open, instead of a hand-written combobox with its own active index and key handler. `Home` and `End` now move the caret in the box rather than jumping to the first and last row
  - Rejected: Keeping the hand-written listbox; Base UI `Combobox`; restoring `Home` and `End` with a key handler around the input
  - Why: The library already keeps the active descendant, the scrolling, pointer highlighting and Enter in step with the ARIA, which the palette had to keep in step by hand, and its own documentation shows this exact command palette. `Combobox` is for choosing a value, while the palette's box is free text whose rows run an action, which is `Autocomplete`'s model. A key handler on the input would bring back the code the change removed, and moving the caret is what `Home` and `End` do in an editable combobox in the ARIA practices
- **2026-10-03.** The Email and Discord sections of Settings › Installation become one "Notification providers" section with an Email and a Discord tab, and the profile's Notifications table shows a channel's column only while that channel is set up on the installation. With no channel set up the table keeps only the in-app column, hides the monthly digest row and its scopes, and has no Save
  - Rejected: Keeping two navigation entries; showing a disabled column for a channel that is not set up, with a note under the table saying why
  - Why: Two entries for the same job go against the fewer-pages direction, and the two forms sit naturally side by side as tabs. The owner found the disabled columns and their notes noisy: a member cannot set up a channel, so a column they cannot use tells them nothing they can act on
- **2026-10-03.** The palette searches transactions by handing off to the ledger: any text adds a last row, "Search transactions for “…”", that opens `/transactions?search=…`. The empty state went with it, since a query can no longer come back empty
  - Rejected: Fetching matching transactions into the palette; putting the row first; offering it only when nothing else matches; a separate search page
  - Why: The ledger's `search` parameter already covers the description, the note, the payee, the place and the receipt items, with paging, sorting and the other filters beside it, so the palette still adds no endpoint and no request per keystroke, as decided on 2026-09-21. First place would make Enter run a transaction search for `tags` instead of opening Tags. Offering it only on no match would hide it whenever a payee also partly matches a category or a page name. A search page would be one more page, against the direction of fewer pages

- **2026-10-02.** Stale content dims to 75% instead of 55%, so ink text keeps at least 6.6:1 and muted text at least 3.1:1 on a section panel in every palette and theme. Decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed
  - Rejected: Keeping 55%, which left light-theme muted text at 2.2:1; 70%, which still left it at 2.9:1; desaturating or recolouring the text instead of fading it
  - Why: 75% is the lowest step of Tailwind's opacity scale that clears 3:1 for muted text everywhere, and ink text still falls from about 14.7:1 to 6.7:1, which reads clearly as faded next to fresh rows. A colour change would need its own value per palette and theme, and a grayscale filter does nothing to text that is already neutral
- **2026-10-02.** The focus ring and the common motion durations are named once in `global.css`: the `focus-ring` and `focus-ring-inset` utilities, and the `duration-quick`, `duration-base`, `duration-view` and `duration-slow` theme tokens. Decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed
  - Rejected: Writing `focus-visible:ring-3 focus-visible:ring-ring/60` at each of eighteen call sites; folding the border colour and the invalid and destructive rings into the utility; a token for every duration in use
  - Why: The ring was identical everywhere it followed the element's own focus, so a change to it meant eighteen edits. The border, invalid and destructive variants differ between controls and must keep winning over the plain ring, which they do as variant classes sorting after a bare utility. Four tokens cover the durations that repeat; exits and single-use view-transition timings stay literal beside their rule

- **2026-10-01.** Ending a session removes the member's own fields from `jx-preferences` (active household, quick add account, command recents, net worth milestones, put-off month close prompt) and keeps the device's (theme, palette, font, text size, sidebar, language, rows per page, hidden amounts). Decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed
  - Rejected: One preferences row per user id; clearing the whole row on sign-out; leaving the row alone
  - Why: The member's fields name their own households, accounts and amounts, so on a shared computer they leaked into the next person's palette and quick add, and an active household they are not in silently fell back to Everything. The device fields describe the screen and the person at it, so clearing them would undo a choice made for the computer. A row per user would keep both apart but needs the user id before the first paint, which the theme script and the language do not have.

- **2026-10-01.** Quick add from the palette opens the ordinary Add transaction dialog filled in, handed over in the router's history state, instead of saving at once with an undo toast. Decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed
  - Rejected: Saving through `POST /api/transactions` straight away with an Undo toast that deletes the row; the draft as search parameters in the URL; a module-level store the transactions page reads
  - Why: The dialog already validates, warns about a closed month, offers the suggested rule and shows the account and the category to correct before anything is written, so one more Enter buys every check the create path has without a second writer; an undo toast would write and then delete, leaving a trash row and an activity entry for a typo. A description in search parameters lands in the address bar and the browser history, which is why the suggestion call is a `POST`. A store outlives Back, so the `n` key would later reopen a stale draft, while history state leaves and returns with its entry
- **2026-10-01.** Quick add's account is the one this browser last saved a new transaction on, `lastAccountId` in `jx-preferences`, then the installation's default account, then the first; the amount and the payee are parsed in the browser, and the payee's first letter is capitalized. Decided while the owner was away, to be reviewed
  - Rejected: The account of the newest transaction in the ledger; the account of the recalled transaction; a column on the user; parsing on the server
  - Why: The newest ledger row is usually a bank import, while quick add is for cash and card payments the bank has not exported yet, so the account last typed by hand is the better guess, and the dialog shows it for correcting. A per-browser preference fits a phone used for the cash wallet and a laptop for the bank, and needs no migration or endpoint. Parsing in the browser costs no request per keystroke, like the rest of the palette's matching
