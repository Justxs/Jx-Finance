# Notifications: decisions

Related: feature page [Notifications](../features/notifications.md); architecture [Background work and notifications](../architecture/background-jobs.md).

## Log

Newest first. Each entry is a choice between real alternatives: what was chosen, what was rejected, and why.

- **2026-09-29.** Open question of the monthly digest plan, decided while the owner was away and to be reviewed: the digest is read in the "Everything" scope only, with no digest per household scope
  - Rejected: One digest per scope the member has closed a month in; a scope picker next to the opt-in
  - Why: "Everything" is what a member sees by default and the union of every household they belong to, so nothing is left out of it, while a household digest would need a new preference, a new deduplication key per scope and repeated figures in several messages. It is the conservative answer: a later choice can add scopes without changing what members already receive
- **2026-09-29.** Open question of the monthly digest plan, decided while the owner was away and to be reviewed: the digest goes out on the first hourly pass of days 1 to 5 that finds none for the member and month, not on a later fixed day and not when the member closes the month
  - Rejected: Waiting until the 5th, when more statements are imported; sending it when the member closes the month
  - Why: This is the plan's own schedule and the reminder's, so both messages share one window and one catch-up rule. Waiting for the 5th trades the summary's timeliness for a shorter open-items list that the member can shorten by importing anyway, and sending on close would leave members who never close a month without a digest, which is who it is most for. The open items say what is still missing, so an early digest is honest about it. Revisit after a real month of use
- **2026-09-29.** A monthly digest is a new `NotificationType.MonthlyDigest` whose opt-in is ticking it for email or Discord; `MonthlyDigestJob` reads `IMonthCloseService.GetMonthAsync` for last month as each subscribed member through `PeriodicJob.RunAsUserAsync`, which resolves `ICurrentUser` to a scoped `JobUser` inside the member's scope and to `HttpCurrentUser` everywhere else; it sends nothing for an empty month and leaves the month-end reminder as it is
  - Rejected: A second summary query for the digest; composing `MonthCloseService` by hand around `AppDbContext.For`, as the budget and unusual-amount jobs compose their smaller services; setting a fake `HttpContext` with the member's claims; a settings field or a switch of its own; a digest in everyone's bell; suppressing the reminder for digest subscribers; ordering movers by percentage change
  - Why: Reading the review itself means the digest cannot disagree with the dashboard. `MonthCloseService` has ten dependencies with their own graphs, so composing it in a job would copy the DI graph and drift from it; the scoped holder is three registrations, changes nothing for a request, where `JobUser.User` is never set, and keeps the existing jobs as they are. A fake `HttpContext` would make background work look like a request to everything that reads one. The notifications table is where each member already chooses kinds and channels, and `MonthClose` gates the review it reads, so no switch was added. A bell-only digest would repeat the dashboard, and the reminder is a to-do for people who close their months while the digest is a summary for anyone. A 400% rise on €2 is noise, so movers are the largest absolute changes, as the dashboard orders categories by amount
- **2026-09-20.** A notification carries a typed `NotificationPayload` in a nullable `jsonb` column, and the mapper rebuilds a bill reminder's payload from the old `Message` text when the column is null
  - Rejected: Keeping the stringly-typed `Title` plus `Message` pair and parsing it per kind on the client; a column per kind; a data migration that rewrites the existing rows into the new shape
  - Why: The pair only worked because there was one kind: a second kind would have had to smuggle a percentage and a period through a sentence, which is exactly the parsing the client already had to do for the due date. A column per kind widens the table for every new producer. A rewrite would have to reparse text written by an older version to produce the same values the mapper can derive on read, with no way back if it got one wrong
- **2026-09-20.** Notifications are not behind a feature switch: the three routes stay out of `FeatureGateMiddleware`, and a row whose producing feature was switched off afterwards stays in the list and in the unread count, while its entry loses its link
  - Rejected: Gating `/api/notifications` on `RecurringBills` as it effectively was; hiding rows whose feature is off; deleting them when the switch flips
  - Why: Notifications are a shared mechanism, so gating them on one producer breaks every other producer. Hiding rows would make the badge jump when an administrator flips a switch and would force the list to know which feature each kind belongs to, and deleting them destroys a record of something that was true. Dropping the link is enough, because the page it opens is not in the navigation either
