# Learned categories

Back to the [feature walkthrough](README.md). See also [decisions](../decisions/learned-categories.md), [categorization rules](categorization-rules.md), [bank statement import](bank-statement-import.md#review), [transactions](transactions.md#suggested-categories).

Backend `Common/LearnedCategories` (`CategoryFeatures`, `CategoryModel`, `ILearnedCategoryService`, `LearnedCategoryService`), `Infrastructure/LearnedCategories` (the evaluation command), `Imports` (`learnedCategoryId` and `learnedConfidence` on preview rows), `Transactions` (`POST /api/transactions/suggest-category`, `GET /api/transactions/uncategorized-suggestions`, `ICategorySuggestionService`, and `onlyUncategorized` on `POST /api/transactions/bulk-category`); frontend the Learned mark of `imports/import-preview-table`, `transactions/category-suggestion` and `transactions/uncategorized-suggestions`. Feature switch `LearnedCategories`, off by default.

Rules cover payees whose text is stable, and the import's recall covers exact repeats among the newest 200 rows. What is left is the long tail: a new branch of a known chain, card lines whose terminal or city changes, a payee last seen a year ago. A small naive Bayes model trained on the categories you already chose guesses those, and only suggests: a rule always wins over it, and it never replaces a category somebody chose.

## Not evaluated yet

The plan made a hold-out evaluation on the owner's own ledger a gate before anything was built on the model. No real ledger existed when the feature was built: the development database holds only `just seed` demo data, where the recall already fills every held-out row. On 2026-10-01 the owner waived the gate and asked for the whole feature, so the switch stays off by default and the constants below are the plan's starting values, not values the evaluation chose. Running `--evaluate-categorizer` on the owner's ledger, once it holds six months and 1,500 categorized rows, comes before the switch is turned on; it is a release-work row in the [backlog](../backlog.md#1-open-release-work). If the model is less precise than the recall there, the order in the import becomes rule, recall, learned; if it fills too few of the rows nothing else fills, the switch stays off.

## Nothing leaves the installation

The model is about 140 lines of C# with no package. It is trained inside the API for each request from the rows the caller can see and thrown away with the request: nothing is stored, cached, logged or sent anywhere, so a model never outlives a deleted row and never reaches a backup. The form suggestion is a `POST` so that a description never lands in a URL or an access log.

## The model

`CategoryModel` is a multinomial naive Bayes with Laplace smoothing. `CategoryFeatures.Of(payeeKey, accountId, amount)` turns a row into tokens: each word of two or more characters of the `PayeeKey` (`SubscriptionDescription.Normalize` of the description, so reference numbers and dates are already gone), the whole key as one `key:` token, `account:<id>`, and `amount:<n>` where `n` is `floor(log2(amount))`, 0 below 2 and at most 20. The whole-key token carries what the recall knows; the words carry the partial matches the recall misses; the account and the amount only matter when history says so, such as a small snack at the shop where the weekly groceries are bought.

`Train` counts tokens per category. `Posteriors` compares only the categories of the row's flow type, so an income row never gets an expense category whatever its words say, ignores tokens it never saw, and normalizes the probabilities so they sum to one. `Predict` returns the best category when its probability is at least `MinimumConfidence` (0.80) and its support is at least `MinimumSupport` (3). Support is the largest number of training rows of that category that contain one of the row's words, so the account and the amount alone never make a guess. Ties break by the lower category id, so the same ledger always gives the same answer.

## What it learns from

`LearnedCategoryService.SuggestAsync` takes a list of candidates (account, flow type, amount, description) and runs one query: the categorized, unsplit transactions with a positive amount the caller can see through `db.Transactions`, dated within `LookBackMonths` (24) of today, newest first, at most `MaxTrainingRows` (10,000), whose category is visible through `db.Categories` and has the row's flow type. It trains on them and predicts every candidate that has a description and a positive amount.

- **Sharing.** A housemate's rows on a shared account count, because on a shared account the useful answer is how the household files it, and the row is one the caller could open anyway. A housemate's personal category is never suggested: such a row's category is not visible to the caller, so the row is left out of the training.
- **Active household.** The query goes through the ordinary filters, so with a household chosen in the switcher only that household's rows train the model.
- **Refunds and splits.** A [refund](transactions.md#refunds) is neither trained on nor guessed for, because the import files a refund in the category of the purchase it refunds. A split parent is left out, because its categories live on its lines, and split lines are never trained on or suggested.
- **The switch.** With `LearnedCategories` off the service answers nothing without querying.

## In the import review

`ImportPreviewService.PreviewAsync` calls the service once, after parsing, for every row that is not a duplicate and got no category from a rule, so every format (Swedbank, camt.053, mapped CSV, OFX and MT940) gets it. A row answers `learnedCategoryId` and `learnedConfidence`, both null when the model abstains. The review then fills each row's category in this order: a rule, then the learned guess, then the recall of the last category with the same description. A rule that only adds tags leaves the category to the learned guess. A learned row shows a "Learned" mark whose tooltip reads "From your history, 93% sure"; choosing another category, "Set category for selected" or recording the row as a refund clears it, as it clears "Suggested category". A refund candidate's category still replaces every suggestion. See [Bank statement import](bank-statement-import.md#review).

## In the transaction form

When the description field loses focus while the category is empty, the row is not split, it is not a refund and the switch is on, the form posts `POST /api/transactions/suggest-category` with the account, the flow type, the amount (0 when it is not a valid amount yet) and the trimmed description. `CategorySuggestionService` asks the caller's rules first while `CategorizationRules` is on, through `ICategorizationRuleService.SuggestAsync`, and then the learned service. The answer is `categoryId`, `source` (`rule` or `learned`), `ruleName` and `confidence`, all null when neither has an opinion; an account the caller cannot see answers 400 `reference.notFound`.

The form shows the answer under the category as a chip, "Suggested: Groceries", with "by rule "Maxima"" or "93% sure" beside it. Clicking it sets the category; nothing is set on its own. The chip is shown only while the category is still empty and the description and the type are the ones the suggestion was asked for, so it disappears when either changes; there is no effect, the call is the blur handler. The call is silent: a failure shows nothing.

## In the ledger

With the Uncategorized filter on and the switch on, the transactions toolbar shows **Suggest categories**. It opens a dialog that reads `GET /api/transactions/uncategorized-suggestions` with the ledger's current filters. The server takes the newest `BulkRules.MaxTransactions` (200) transactions matching them that have no category and are not split, asks the caller's rules per account and then the learned service for the rest, and answers only the rows that got a suggestion, each with the whole transaction, the category, the source and the rule's name or the confidence.

The dialog groups them by category and source: one group per category the model suggested, labelled "From your history", and one per rule, labelled with the rule, each with its count, a checkbox and its rows (date, payee or description, amount, and the confidence of a learned row). **Apply** sends one `POST /api/transactions/bulk-category` per ticked group with `onlyUncategorized: true` and toasts the total of the `updated` counts. With `onlyUncategorized` the bulk call changes only the listed rows that still have no category, so a row somebody categorized between the review and the apply keeps its category, and `updated` counts only the rows that changed. The suggestions query is made stale by every mutation that refreshes the ledger.

## The switch

`LearnedCategories`, shown as **Learned categories** among the ledger features, starts off (`FeatureFlags.Default`, and `false` for an existing installation through the `AddLearnedCategoriesSwitch` migration of 2026-10-01). It gates `GET /api/transactions/uncategorized-suggestions` on its endpoint, inside the ungated Transactions prefix, like `Locations`; with it off that route answers 404 `feature.disabled`, `POST /api/transactions/suggest-category` answers only rules, the preview's learned fields are null and the form and the toolbar offer nothing. The suggestion route has had no gate of its own since 2026-10-01, so the command palette's [quick add](interface.md#command-palette) finds a rule with the switch off; quick add takes a rule, then the recall, then a learned guess. It works with `CategorizationRules` off: the form and the ledger then suggest from the model alone. `GET /api/transactions/uncategorized-suggestions` is readable with a personal API token like the other ledger reads; the form's `POST` is browser-only. See [Installation settings](installation-settings.md).

## Evaluating on a ledger

**The command.** In the container, `docker compose run --rm --no-deps api --evaluate-categorizer member@example.com`; on the development machine, with `ConnectionStrings__Default` pointing at the database, `dotnet run --project backend/JxFinance.Api -c Release --no-launch-profile -- --evaluate-categorizer member@example.com`. Appending `;Options=-c default_transaction_read_only=on` to the connection string makes PostgreSQL refuse any write. `Program.cs` starts `CategorizerEvaluationCommand` before the pipeline, like `--recover-admin`: it applies no migration, starts no job and writes nothing. It works for any member, administrator or not, and needs no terminal.

**What it reads.** The command runs as that member through `JobUser`, so it sees what the member sees in the ledger: their own accounts and the accounts shared with them, in every household. It reads categorized, unsplit transactions with a positive amount whose category is visible and has the row's flow type, the rows the service trains on.

**The split.** The newest such row sets the end. Rows of the three months up to it are held out; the newest 10,000 rows of the 24 months before that train the model. For every held-out row the command also replays what exists today: the member's current rules in position order through `RuleMatcher`, where the first match decides and a first match that only adds tags gives no category, and the recall, which takes the newest training row with the same trimmed, lower-cased description and flow type among the 200 newest. The import's recall reads the 200 newest rows of the ledger, uncategorized ones included, so the replay is slightly kinder to the recall than the product is.

**What it prints.** Counts and percentages only, never a description, a category name, an account or a date. One table gives rows, filled, coverage and precision for the rules, the recall, the model at 0.80, the order without the model (rule, then recall), the order with it (rule, learned, recall), the model on the rows that neither a rule nor the recall fills, and the recall and the model on the rows the recall fills. A second table repeats the model for thresholds from 0.60 to 0.95, on those unfilled rows and on all held-out rows. Then come the time to train and to predict every held-out row, feature extraction included and the query not, taken from the second of two runs so that just-in-time compilation is not counted, and one pass or fail line for each of the first three conditions below.

**What has to hold before the switch goes on.** The ledger holds at least six months and 1,500 categorized, unsplit rows; on the last three months, at the chosen threshold, learned guesses on the rows that neither a rule nor the recall fills are right at least 90% of the time and cover at least 30% of them; on the rows the recall fills the model is at least as precise as the recall; training plus prediction for a 500-row preview takes under 150 ms and the form suggestion under 100 ms on the production host (an in-memory cache keyed by user and ledger is the first fallback otherwise); and after a month with the switch on, fewer than 1 in 20 accepted learned guesses were changed afterwards. On 10,000 synthetic rows in 40 categories the development laptop trained in 31 to 72 ms and trained plus predicted 500 rows in 82 to 124 ms, the query not included.

## Tests

`CategoryFeaturesTests` and `CategoryModelTests` cover the tokens and the model: a word learned from rows whose other words differ, the account deciding a tie, the support floor and the threshold, only the row's flow type competing, posteriors summing to one and a stable tie-break. `CategorizerEvaluationTests` runs the command on demo data and checks that no description or category name reaches the output. `LearnedCategoryImportTests` checks that a row no rule fills gets a guess, a ruled row and a duplicate none, and nothing with the switch off. `CategorySuggestionTests` covers the form suggestion (learned, a rule winning, nothing, an unknown account, the switch off), sharing (a housemate's rows train the guess, their personal category is never suggested, the active household narrows the rows) and the ledger review (at most 200 rows, newest first, never split or categorized rows, the ledger filter, the switch off). `TransactionSummaryAndBulkCategoryEndpointTests` checks that `onlyUncategorized` skips a row categorized after the review. On the frontend, `preview-rows.test.ts` covers the rule, learned, recall order and clearing the mark, and the `CategorySuggestion`, `UncategorizedSuggestionsDialog`, `TransactionsToolbar`, `TransactionForm` and `ImportPreviewTable` stories cover the chip, the dialog and the mark.
