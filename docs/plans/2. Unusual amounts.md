# Plan: Unusual-amount flags and subscription price rises

Status: planned 2026-09-25. Size M. Build this after [Discord notifications](<1. Discord notifications.md>), because its two new notification kinds should reach Discord through the publisher.

## Outcome

- An expense far above what its payee or its category usually costs gets a badge in the ledger and in the import preview. The badge carries a sentence such as "3.1× the usual 42.00 EUR at Maxima".
- Newly recorded unusual rows raise a notification.
- The ledger can show unusual rows only. A row can be marked "not unusual", and then stays quiet.
- When a subscription that has a recurring entry is charged more than the entry expects, the recurring entries page says so, a notification is raised, and one button updates the expected amount.

## Decisions

| Topic | Decision | Rejected | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| When it is computed | A background job evaluates each new or edited expense once and stores the result on the transaction. The import preview calls the same evaluator synchronously for rows not stored yet | Computing per page on read | Reading needs 12 months of history per row and cannot filter or sort. Stored results make `unusual=true` a plain indexed filter. The notification needs a moment of "newly flagged" anyway |
| What it means | "Unusual compared with the 12 months before its date, as of when it was recorded or last edited." Later changes to the history do not re-flag old rows | Re-evaluating everything nightly | Stable flags that do not flicker; this is written down in the feature doc |
| Which rows | Expenses only; non-split rows only in v1. Transfers, conversions and investment entries are never flagged | Income; split lines | A split has no per-line reporting amount stored. Income spikes (a bonus) are rarely a problem to catch |
| Baseline | First the payee: the same account plus the same `SubscriptionDescription.Normalize` key, with at least 4 earlier occurrences. Otherwise the category: the same category on accounts visible to the account owner, with at least 8 occurrences | Category only | "Unusual for this shop" is sharper than "unusual for groceries", and it reuses the normalizer subscription detection already trusts |
| Statistic | Median and scaled median absolute deviation (MAD × 1.4826) of `ReportingAmount`. A row is flagged when `amount ≥ 2 × median`, `amount ≥ median + 4 × spread` and `amount − median ≥ 10` reporting units. All three constants live in one pure class | Mean and standard deviation | Robust to the outliers it is looking for; the rule is readable in one line |
| Switch | New `Feature.UnusualAmounts`, default on | No switch, like tags | It runs a job and raises notifications; an installation should be able to stop both |
| Who is notified | The account owner | Every household member | Matches bills and budgets, which are personal |
| Linking entries to payments | `RecurringBill.MatchKey` (a normalized description), filled when an entry is created from a subscription suggestion and editable. When empty, the entry's normalized name is used, as `CoveredAsync` does today | A foreign key on transactions | Imported bank rows are the real charges, and they never pass through confirmation. A confirmed fixed entry always writes the expected amount, so it cannot show a rise |

## Data model

| Change | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| `Transaction.UnusualCheckedAt?` | `DateTimeOffset`. Null means not evaluated yet. `AppDbContext.ApplyEntityRules` resets it to null when `Amount`, `Date`, `CategoryId`, `Description`, `AccountId` or `Type` change on a tracked transaction. That is the only hook the write paths need |
| `Transaction.Unusual?` | Complex type with `Basis` (Payee/Category), `TypicalAmount`, `Factor` and `SampleSize`. Null when the row is usual |
| `Transaction.UnusualDismissedAt?` | Set by "Not unusual". Kept across edits so a corrected typo does not bring the flag back |
| Index | Partial index on `(AccountId, Date)` where `Unusual` is not null and not dismissed, for the filter. Partial index on `UnusualCheckedAt` where null, for the job |
| `RecurringBill.MatchKey?` | max 200 |
| `NotificationType.UnusualAmount`, `UnusualAmounts`, `RecurringPriceRise` | Appended. The payload gains `TransactionId?`, `Amount?`, `TypicalAmount?`, `Factor?`, `Count?`, `BillId?`. `NotificationRelated.Transaction` is added |
| `Feature.UnusualAmounts` | `HasDefaultValue(true)` |

The migration leaves existing rows with `UnusualCheckedAt = null`, so the job backfills them in pages and raises no notifications for them (see step 3).

## Backend steps

1. **Pure rules.** `Common/Unusual/UnusualAmountRule` holds the constants and `Evaluate(decimal amount, IReadOnlyList<decimal> history, basis)`, which returns `UnusualVerdict?`. Unit-test it with theories: too little history, flat history, noisy history, the floor, exactly on each boundary, and negative or zero medians.
2. **Evaluator.** `Common/Unusual/IUnusualAmountService.EvaluateAsync(IReadOnlyList<UnusualCandidate>)` returns verdicts in input order, the way `ICategorizationRuleService.SuggestAsync` does. A candidate is account, category, date, reporting amount and description. It loads one bounded five-column projection per account set: non-split expenses in the 12 months before the latest candidate, capped at 5000 rows. It groups in memory by payee key and by category, and excludes each candidate from its own history. `SubscriptionDetectionService` keeps its own query; the two share only `SubscriptionDescription`.
3. **Job.** `UnusualAmountJob : PeriodicJob` runs every 15 minutes with `RequiredFeature = UnusualAmounts` and an `AppLock.UnusualAmounts` lock. It pages 500 unchecked expenses at a time with `IgnoreQueryFilters(OwnerOnly)`, grouped by account owner, and evaluates each group through `AppDbContext.For(services, ownerId)`. It stores the verdict and `UnusualCheckedAt`, then notifies:
   - only for rows newly flagged, not dismissed, dated within the last 45 days and created after the feature was deployed. This keeps the backfill silent;
   - through `INotificationPublisher`, one `UnusualAmount` per row, deduplicated by `RelatedId`;
   - when a pass flags more than three rows for one owner, a single `UnusualAmounts` notification with `Count` instead.
4. **Price rise.** In the same pass, each newly checked expense whose `(AccountId, normalized description)` matches an active expense `RecurringBill`'s `MatchKey` (or its normalized name) is compared with the entry. It is a rise when the amount exceeds the expected `Amount` (fixed) or the median of earlier matches (variable) by more than 3% and 0.50. That raises `RecurringPriceRise`, deduplicated by bill and transaction. It is compared in the transaction's own currency when that matches the bill's, and skipped otherwise.
5. **Ledger.**
   - `TransactionResponse` gains `Unusual?` (basis, typical amount in the reporting currency, factor) and `UnusualDismissed`.
   - `GetTransactionsRequest` gains `Unusual` (bool). It keeps rows flagged and not dismissed.
   - Exports gain no column in v1.
   - Endpoint `POST /api/transactions/{id}/unusual/dismiss` and `DELETE` of the same route to undo it, under `TransactionsGroup` and gated by the new feature through `RequiresFeature` metadata on the two endpoints.
6. **Import preview.** `ImportService.PreviewSwedbankCsvAsync` makes one more batch call, `EvaluateAsync`, over the expense rows when the feature is on. It uses the rows' reporting amounts at the preview's rates and adds `Unusual?` to `ImportPreviewRow`. Confirmed rows are still evaluated by the job, so the stored flag has one source.
7. **Recurring entries.**
   - `RecurringBillResponse` gains `MatchKey` and `LatestMatch?` (date, amount, `IsPriceRise`). These are read in one query over the page's entries through `(AccountId, normalized description)`, bounded to the last 13 months.
   - The create form sends `MatchKey` when it comes from a suggestion (`SubscriptionCandidateResponse.Description` is already the normalized key).
   - The edit form shows it as "Matches bank text".
   - "Update expected amount" uses the existing update endpoint; no new write path.
8. **Error codes:** none new beyond `resource.notFound` for dismiss.

## Frontend steps

1. `just gen`. List the dismiss and undismiss mutations in `invalidation.ts` against the transactions root.
2. **Badge.** `features/transactions/unusual-amount/unusual-amount-badge.tsx` shows a lucide `TrendingUp` icon with `title` and an `sr-only` sentence, meaning carried by text and never by colour alone. It is rendered next to `AttachmentCount` in `use-transaction-columns.tsx`, `transactions-list.tsx` and `recent-transactions-list.tsx`. Stories cover payee basis, category basis and dismissed.
3. **Row action.** "Not unusual" and "Mark as unusual again" go in the row menu with the undo toast pattern.
4. **Filter.** An "Unusual only" toggle in the ledger filters, added to the route's zod search and to the saved filter shape. Old saved filters parse without it.
5. **Import.** An "Unusual" `Tag` chip in `import-row.tsx`'s flags block, with the sentence in its tooltip. `preview-rows.ts` carries the field.
6. **Recurring page.**
   - A price-rise notice on `recurring-bill-row.tsx`: "Charged 12.99 on 3 Sep, expected 9.99", with an Update button.
   - A `MatchKey` field in the bill form.
7. **Bell.** Three new `producers` entries and `describe()` texts in en/lt. `NotificationTexts` on the server gets the same three, for Discord.
8. **Settings.** The feature switch in `features-fields.tsx` and the storybook settings fixture.

## Tests

- **Unit:** `UnusualAmountRule` theories; the `ApplyEntityRules` reset only on the listed fields.
- **Integration:**
  - A payee baseline flags a row 3× its usual amount. A category baseline is used when the payee is new. Too little history flags nothing.
  - Split rows, income and transfers are never flagged.
  - Editing the amount back to normal clears the flag on the next run, and a dismissal survives the edit.
  - The backfill raises no notifications.
  - More than three flags make one summary notification, and running twice notifies once.
  - The ledger filter and paging, and visibility through a shared account.
  - The preview flag matches what the job stores for the same row after confirmation.
  - Price rise for fixed and variable entries, a match through `MatchKey` and through the name, and currency mismatch skipped.
  - Switching the feature off stops the job and hides the badge fields, which read as null.
